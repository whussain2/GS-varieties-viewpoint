POPULATION IMPROVEMENT
Goal: raise the frequency of + alleles, as fast as possible. Output: better parents.
Why GS belongs here
ΔG per year = i · r · σA / L
i ↑
Finds high breeding-value lines among thousands of untested lines: genotyping a line costs less than a yield plot, so more candidates and stronger selection.
r
Higher than visual or single-plot selection of early-generation lines for a low-heritability trait like yield, though usually lower than replicated yield trials. The net gain comes through i and L.
L ↓
Shorter cycle: parents are chosen before yield testing, so the + alleles are recombined again sooner.
σA
Keep enough parents per cycle, or good alleles are lost by chance (panel C).

PRODUCT DEVELOPMENT
Goal: find the fixed line that performs best for farmers in the target population of environments (TPE). Output: a variety.
Stagelineshow tested (typical)
1 · Observational / preliminary trial~1,0001–2 sites, unreplicated; year 1
2 · Advanced yield trial~1503–5 sites, replicated, with checks; year 2
3 · Multi-environment trials in the TPE~158–15 sites, 2–3 seasons, vs. checks; years 3–4
4 · National / registration trials2–3official trials, DUS; years 5–6
Release1seed multiplication, adoption
What decides a variety
Yield against checks across the TPE, stability and G×E, grain quality, disease and stress tolerance, and registration rules. These are judged on a fixed line's own field performance across the TPE. GEBVs can help cull lines at stage 1, but the release decision rests on field trials.
Genomic selection's job is in population improvement: rank parents, cut cycle time, raise good-allele frequency. Product development identifies the variety, in the field.
Stage sizes and durations are typical of public inbred-crop programs and vary by crop. Concept after Gaynor et al. (2017) and Cobb et al. (2019).
